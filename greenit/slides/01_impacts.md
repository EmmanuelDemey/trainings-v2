# 01 - The environmental impacts of digital

---

# Learning objectives

At the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- **Give** the orders of magnitude of the footprint of digital technology
- **Tell** where it comes from: the **devices**, the **networks**, the **data centres** —
  and **making** them versus **using** them
- **Explain** the ecological backpack of a device
- **List** the social impacts, not only the environmental ones
- **Describe** what eco-design is, and what it is not

---

# Digital is material

<div style="display: flex; gap: 2em;">
<div>

### Three tiers

- **Devices** — phones, laptops, screens, TVs, connected objects: the
  largest part, by far
- **Networks** — fixed and mobile, the boxes, the antennas
- **Data centres** — servers, storage, cooling

</div>
<div>

### Four phases

- **Extracting** the raw materials — metals, rare earths
- **Making** the components and the devices
- **Using** them — electricity
- **End of life** — collection, recycling… or not

</div>
</div>

<br />

> The cloud is a **building full of computers**, somewhere.

# Orders of magnitude

| Scope | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| World — share of greenhouse gas emissions | **≈ 2 to 4 %** | The Shift Project, GreenIT.fr |
| France — share of the carbon footprint (2022) | **4.4 %** | ADEME–Arcep, 2025 |
| France — share of the footprint due to **devices** | **≈ 50 %** | ADEME–Arcep, 2025 |
| France — share due to **data centres** | **≈ 46 %** | ADEME–Arcep, 2025 |
| World — electricity of data centres (2024) | **≈ 1.5 %**, ~415 TWh, growing fast with AI | IEA, *Energy and AI*, 2025 |

<br />

> Comparable to air transport — and growing, where most sectors must shrink.

# Making weighs more than using

For a **smartphone** or a **laptop**, most of the footprint is spent **before it
is switched on**: extracting, refining, making, shipping.

- Typically **around 80 %** of a smartphone's carbon footprint comes from its
  manufacture (ADEME)
- The electricity of its use is small — and, in France, low-carbon
- So the **first lever** is not the energy: it is **keeping devices longer**,
  and **not forcing anyone to replace theirs**

<br />

> A site that needs a recent phone to run **makes** phones obsolete.

# The ecological backpack

The **ecological backpack** (*sac à dos écologique*, MIPS) is the mass of raw
materials moved to make an object, beyond its own weight.

<div style="display: flex; gap: 2em;">
<div>

### A smartphone

- About **150 g** in your hand
- Tens of **kilograms** of materials moved to make it — the ADEME gives
  **≈ 70 kg**
- Dozens of metals, some of them rare or from conflict zones

</div>
<div>

### Why it matters

- Mining: water, energy, pollution, **non-renewable** resources
- **Abiotic resource depletion** is often the first impact of digital, ahead
  of carbon
- Recycling recovers only part of the metals

</div>
</div>

<!--
The figure is an order of magnitude: it varies with the method. The message: the object hides a mass a few hundred times its own.
-->

# More than carbon

A **life-cycle assessment** (LCA) counts several impact categories at once:

- **Climate change** — kgCO₂e
- **Abiotic resource depletion** — metals and minerals
- **Water** — consumed by mines, factories and data-centre cooling
- **Energy** — primary energy
- **Ionising radiation, ecotoxicity**…

<br />

> Optimising carbon alone can **shift** the impact elsewhere: a low-carbon
> data centre that drinks the water of a dry region.

<!--
The reference method in Europe is the PEF (Product Environmental Footprint). Mention it, do not detail it.

# The social impacts

- **Working conditions** in the mines and the factories
- **Conflict minerals** — tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold
- **E-waste** exported, dismantled by hand, burnt in the open
- **Digital exclusion** — about one adult in three lacks digital skills in
  France (Insee): a heavy, complex service **leaves people out**
- **Attention** — dark patterns, infinite scroll, notifications

<br />

> Responsible digital = **environmental** + **social**. Accessibility is part of it.

<!--
Link with the accessibility training if people took it: a light, simple, accessible service works on an old phone, on a poor network, for more people.
-->

# How digital evolves

- **More devices** per person, and more **connected objects**
- **Heavier services**: the median web page weighs about **2 MB** today,
  several times more than ten years ago (HTTP Archive)
- **Video** dominates the traffic of the networks
- **AI** — the new driver of the growth of data centres (chapter 04)
- The **rebound effect** (Jevons): each efficiency gain is eaten by more uses

<br />

> Efficiency alone does not reduce the footprint. **Sobriety** does: doing
> less, on purpose.

<!--
Efficiency: the same service with fewer resources. Sobriety: questioning the service itself. Eco-design needs both.
-->

---

# Eco-design

**Eco-design** (*écoconception*) of a digital service: reducing its
environmental impacts **over its whole life cycle**, from the **need** to the
**end of life**, while keeping the service it renders.

<div style="display: flex; gap: 2em;">
<div>

### It is

- Questioning the **need** first — the most effective lever
- Designing for **old devices** and **poor networks**
- Measuring, then improving, **continuously**
- A **team** matter: product, design, development, hosting

</div>
<div>

### It is not

- Only optimising images at the end
- Only choosing a "green" host
- Only carbon offsetting
- A label to stick on at the end

</div>
</div>
