# 08 - How to convince

---

# Learning objectives

At the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- **Argue** for eco-design with each audience: management, product, developers
- **Anticipate** the usual objections
- **Build** a roadmap that starts small and lasts

---

# One argument per audience

| Audience | What speaks to them |
|---|---|
| **Management** | the law (REEN, CSRD), the brand, the **costs** of hosting and of the cloud |
| **Product** | **speed**, conversion, the users on old phones and poor networks, accessibility |
| **Developers** | performance, simplicity, less code to maintain, **technical debt** |
| **Users** | data plans, battery, devices that last |
| **CSR / sustainability** | the carbon reporting, the scope 3 |

# The usual objections

- **"Our footprint is negligible."** — Multiply by the views, and count the
  devices the service makes obsolete.
- **"The users want rich features."** — Do they? Measure what is used.
- **"It will cost more."** — Less code, less hosting, faster pages: it usually
  costs less, after the first audit.
- **"Our host is green."** — Hosting is a small part; the devices are the largest.
- **"We will offset."** — Offsetting does not reduce the footprint, and the
  law restricts carbon-neutrality claims.

# Start small, make it last

1. **One** service, **one** audit, a **baseline**
2. **Quick wins**, measured, and **shown**
3. A **budget** in the CI
4. **Train** the team — designers and product owners too
5. A **referent** and a **ritual**: a review of the measures per release
6. The **statement of eco-design**, published and updated
7. Then the **product** decisions: what to stop

<br />

> Make the footprint **visible**: what is measured and shown gets improved.
